For your 2027 planning, we’re sharing two reports: what SaaS cancellation feedback suggests about product habits, and what ecommerce discount tests reveal after an offer is accepted.

01 / SaaS retention trends

Use cancellation feedback to improve product habits.

In our 2026 SaaS sample, cancellation answers included difficulty building a habit, lack of time, and needs that had ended or changed.

For customers who still need the product but struggle to use it regularly, test whether helping them repeat one useful action builds a habit. It also explores how to introduce a relevant next capability after a customer completes their first goal.

A customer who has finished a one-time project may have little reason to continue. Use the feedback to distinguish that situation from a customer who needs help finding ongoing value.

02 / Ecommerce retention trends

Check what happens after a discount is accepted.

Our ecommerce report examines a small set of merchants, including cancellation reasons, temporary breaks, and randomized discount tests.

In one test, 50% off won more acceptances than 40% off, with almost no additional recorded revenue over 90 days. Another test produced more revenue with the deeper discount. The results show why each offer needs its own follow-up.

The report also explores testing a pause for a temporary interruption, where the subscription platform supports it.

Put the findings to work

Use cancellation reasons to choose a product or lifecycle change to test. When testing an offer, compare it with your current approach and follow subsequent payments, cancellations, and offer costs before expanding it.

These findings describe the accounts studied, not industry-wide benchmarks. The SaaS sample includes consumer apps and business software. Ecommerce revenue is retention-attributed, not complete revenue or margin. See each report for its methods and limitations.
